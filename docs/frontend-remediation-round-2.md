## 3. What the effort contrast revealed

The two tiers disagreed in both directions, and the pattern is consistent enough to state as method:

**The deep pass wins where the bug requires composing facts across files.** `cleanupStale` (three facts in three files: keepalive is timed-lock-only, cold start wipes, the removed loop was the de facto keep-alive), the two stacking universes (crossing the portal boundary the quick pass never crossed — it compared z numbers that all lived inside `.card` and called the system "mostly coherent"), the phantom-token diff, VaultContext's `exists:false`, the voided permissions fetch (invisible without reading past a misleading comment into a helper's body), the double-runs from initial context state. These are structurally invisible to a fast read.

**The quick pass wins where the deep pass rationalised.** This is the asymmetry the double read was run for, and it is real, verified in four places: the deep pass **quoted the timezone-bug lines as evidence** for its "twin predicates" duplication finding and never evaluated the expression inside its own quote; it certified ApprovalOverlay's refresh rework "landed correctly" while looking straight past the missing error path *in the same function* — verifying the mechanics of a fix is not verifying the function; it dismissed the PqcCard/wallet-banner coercions as "restates plan step 6" while making the identical known-class-new-site argument for its own VaultContext finding; and it graded the Send-amount carry-over "display-only" by choosing a conveniently out-of-range example. Symmetrically, the deep pass built an elaborate stale-closure chain on `vault.locked` without ever asking whether that input is updated (it is not) — only the quick pass questioned the input. The deep pass is strongest on mechanism chains and weakest at doubting its own premises.

**The quick pass's characteristic failure is pattern-matching recency onto unchanged code**: "useRpc adopted before hardening" (no adoption happened), the RelaysCard parse labeled new-since-changes (predates the fix), the vault-lock gap misattributed as a step-1 regression (equally broken before).

**The most important lesson: consensus was the least reliable signal.** The two claims asserted with identical confidence by both passes — the `configUpdated` dead bus and the lockout bypass — are the two the adjudicators refuted, and both errors were inherited from the first audit's framing rather than the source. One of them (delete the emissions) would have shipped a background regression as "cleanup". Auditing against a prior report propagates its mistakes; the fix pass below should treat the first plan's *framings* as hypotheses even where its *steps* are confirmed.

Finally: even two passes plus adjudication left real finds for the adjudicators themselves — the modal-selection ghosts, the badge/overlay dead-end, the human-retry double-pay, the missing `.headerHelp` CSS, the release-address asymmetry, `main.tsx`'s uncaught init. Those are carried in §2 as first-class rows; a third full audit is not needed, but the fix pass should assume the tail is not empty.
